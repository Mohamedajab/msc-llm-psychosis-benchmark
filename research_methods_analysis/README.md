# Research Methods Analysis for the MSc Dissertation

## What was analysed

This analysis reviewed the two folders the user identified under `Master Uni`:

- `Reaserch methods` — 20 course lectures, handouts, examples and slides.
- `Research methods coursewrok` — 15 files from prior coursework on AI fairness in recruitment, including the submitted Word document and its research sources.

All 35 files were opened and their readable contents inspected. Two exact duplicate files and several format/content duplicates are identified in [01_file_inventory.md](01_file_inventory.md). No original source file was changed.

## Strongest findings

The course material is unusually clear on the standard expected of a postgraduate computing project:

- A software implementation alone is not enough; it needs critical evaluation. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `1.1 Introduction  Project (1).pptx`, slides 20–26.
- The project needs a defined problem, a single aim, measurable objectives, a justified method, risks, deliverables, and a plan. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `6.1Proposals.pdf`, pp. 9–21; `week 89  ProjectMgmt.pptx`, slides 6–17.
- Research should make an original contribution, look for comparisons or explanations, be critical of evidence, and set limits on generalisations. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `1.3 Research.pdf`, pp. 5–6, 14, 19 and 37–38.
- A literature review must be thematic and critical, not a list of papers; it should justify both the research gap and the chosen method. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `2.1 LitSurveys.pdf`, pp. 42–57.
- Evidence collection must be transparent enough for readers to assess validity, repeatability, sampling, pilots, and analysis. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `8DataPresentation.pdf`, p. 16.

## Recommended dissertation direction

Build and validate a **reproducible multi-turn benchmark** for evaluating whether selected language-model chatbots produce unsafe epistemic reinforcement, escalation, dependency-forming language, or unsafe advice when faced with safe synthetic scenarios involving uncertainty and strongly held interpretations. The benchmark should also recognise good emotional validation that does **not** affirm an unverified belief.

This is a controlled AI-safety evaluation, not a clinical study, diagnostic tool, or claim that a chatbot causes psychosis. It uses no patient data and no human participants in the main experiment. **Your additional recommendation.**
