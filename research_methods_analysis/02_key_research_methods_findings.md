# Key Research Methods Findings and Application Rules

## Direct findings from the supplied material

### 1. The dissertation needs a research contribution, not just an implementation

Research is described as a considered activity making an original contribution to knowledge; it should investigate explanations, relationships, comparisons, predictions or theories, rather than only describe information. The lecture lists new tools, techniques, procedures and methods as possible forms of originality. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `1.3 Research.pdf`, pp. 5–6 and 14.

For a computing project, development of software or hardware still needs evaluation at postgraduate level. Evaluation may use experiments to compare techniques, systems, processes or protocols in different situations. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `1.1 Introduction  Project (1).pptx`, slides 22–23.

**Application:** the contribution must be the benchmark specification, safe scenario design, validated scoring rubric, reproducible evaluation pipeline and comparative evidence—not simply a script that calls model APIs. **Adapted from the Research Methods material.**

### 2. Research questions, objectives, method and outcome must join up

A proposal should contain a title, introduction, aim/objectives, related research, gap, risks, Gantt plan and references. Research methods should explain how objectives will be achieved, including samples, data gathering and analysis; expected deliverables should be explicit. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `6.1Proposals.pdf`, pp. 13, 18–21.

Objectives should be specific, measurable, assignable, realistic and time-related; they are measurable sub-goals, not a list of work activities. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `week 89  ProjectMgmt.pptx`, slides 9–14.

**Application:** every research question below has a named measure, source of evidence and analysis. The coding pipeline and benchmark are therefore necessary deliverables, not optional extras. **Adapted from the Research Methods material.**

### 3. A good literature review is a critical, structured argument

The course distinguishes literature searching from literature reviewing. The search should have a strategy, keywords/synonyms, limits, recorded searches and complete bibliographic details. The review should identify a gap, criticise logic and limitations, and assess research question, hypotheses, design, measures, samples and conclusions. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `2.1 LitSurveys.pdf`, pp. 7–9, 18–30 and 42–45.

Reviews should be organised by topic, not source-by-source; they should select relevant material, justify assertions, and synthesise a conclusion. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `2.1 LitSurveys.pdf`, pp. 49–57; `LiteratureReviews.pdf`, pp. 3–8.

**Application:** use a documented search protocol and organise the dissertation review by risks, multi-turn safety, benchmark design, annotation, evaluation and ethics—not by author or model vendor. **Adapted from the Research Methods material.**

### 4. Validity, repeatability and analysis must be visible

The data-presentation lecture says readers should be able to see whether data collection is valid and repeatable, including the data-collection method, sample size/selection, pilots or tests, and analysis/statistics where appropriate. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `8DataPresentation.pdf`, p. 16.

Good research examines data critically and both generalises and places limits on its generalisations. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `1.3 Research.pdf`, p. 19.

**Application:** report exact model identifiers, prompts, configurations, dates, seeds where supported, retries, scenario IDs, annotation rules, excluded runs and limitations. Treat conclusions as applying only to the selected models, configurations and synthetic scenarios. **Adapted from the Research Methods material.**

### 5. The research process is iterative, but changes must be documented

The course presents an evolutionary model in which questions, feasible methods, ethical constraints and sufficient evidence can change as research evolves; it cautions that this does not mean there are no rules. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `1.3 Research.pdf`, pp. 27–31.

The proposal is a basis for later evaluation and may be modified as the project develops. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `6.1Proposals.pdf`, p. 9.

**Application:** conduct a pilot, revise the benchmark once, lock a versioned main-study protocol, and report every post-pilot change. Do not silently tune scenarios or scoring after seeing the main results. **Adapted from the Research Methods material.**

### 6. Academic writing is evidence-led and evaluative

The report-writing material says two key features of a good academic report are justification for choices and evaluation of whether choices were right; every substantive statement needs evidence. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `3.1 WritingReports.pdf`, pp. 42–44 and 47–50.

The recommended experimental report structure is Introduction, Background/Literature Review, Problem Statement/Hypothesis, Methods, Experiments/Data Gathering, Results, Conclusions, References and Appendices. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `3.1 WritingReports.pdf`, p. 5.

**Application:** the final dissertation should justify scenario categories, rubric weights, model selection and statistics, then separately evaluate their weaknesses. **Adapted from the Research Methods material.**

### 7. Information quality and ethical AI use matter

The librarian session recommends a planned strategy, academic databases, abstract screening, citation searching and PROMPT evaluation: Presentation, Relevance, Objectivity, Method, Provenance and Timeliness. It also requires transparent acknowledgement of generative-AI use where permitted, preserved interaction records, and critical checking against credible sources. **Taken from the Research Methods material** — `COP500_Research methods_C Greasley_2025.pptx`, slides 4–8, 25–34 and 52–60.

**Application:** use ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Scopus/Web of Science, PubMed/Medline and PsycINFO where available; declare any AI assistance in accordance with the dissertation policy and keep a private audit trail. **Adapted from the Research Methods material.**

## Transferable insights from the supplementary coursework

The recruitment coursework repeatedly shows that the label being measured is a value-laden construct, and that an audit should distinguish technical metrics, governance, transparency and real-world limits. `Research methods 21.12.25.docx` also criticises overreliance on public vendor information and shallow source-by-source summaries (sections 3.2–3.6 and Deliverable 2). **Taken from the supplied coursework material, not from the COP500 teaching material.**

**Application:** define “reinforcement”, “safe emotional validation” and “unsafe advice” before running models; do not treat a composite score as an objective clinical truth. **Your additional recommendation.**

Synthetic data can support a testbed when the constructed bias/risk and scenario protocols are explicit, as illustrated by `Pena_Bias_in_Multimodal_AI_Testbed_for_Fair_Automatic_Recruitment_CVPRW_2020_paper.pdf`, sections 3–5. **Taken from the supplied coursework material.**

**Application:** create transparent synthetic conversation templates and neutral controls, then release only the level of detail approved by ethics/safeguarding review. **Adapted from the supplied coursework material.**

## Requirements absent from the material

The files do not provide a dissertation-specific marking rubric, approved ethics procedure, word limit, model-provider policy, or the programme's allowed use of AI for the final dissertation. These must be checked with the supervisor and current programme documentation. **Audit observation.**
